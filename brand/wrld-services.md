# WRLD.Services — Managed IT / MSP Brief

> Load `brand/CLAUDE.md` first.

**Service branch.** WRLD.Services (operationally "WRLD.Systems MSP") — managed IT, network security, cloud computing, data backup, endpoint management, vendor management.
**Companion service.** WRLD.Support — 24/7 technical support and troubleshooting, tied to SLA commitments.
**Audience.** SMB IT contacts, internal ops leads, office managers wearing the IT hat, executives approving MSP contracts.

## Tone dial

Warmth **Medium** · Formality **Medium** · Technicality **Medium-High** · Urgency **Medium-High**.

Reassuring and operationally confident. When something breaks, this is the brand that answers the phone — copy should feel like the voice on the other end of that call.

## Color weighting

Monochromatic base. Status / alert indicators use system semantic colors (not brand accents) — green for healthy, amber for warning, red for critical. Interactive brand accent on hover is `#007fee`. Commerce (upgrade plan, add endpoint) uses `#EE9300`.

## Positioning

> "24/7 managed IT with real humans, guaranteed response times, and a team that treats your infrastructure like our own."

Emphasis points:
- **Proactive, not reactive.** Monitoring, patching, planning.
- **Guaranteed response SLAs.** Specific time commitments.
- **US-based, human support.** Not offshore call center. Not a bot.
- **Vendor-neutral.** We choose the right tool, not the commissioned one.

## Must-haves

- SLA matrix with concrete response-time commitments (Critical / High / Medium / Low).
- Monitoring / coverage scope description.
- Escalation path — who to call, when, how.
- Pricing orientation: per-endpoint, per-user, or flat — whichever applies to the offering.
- Link to WRLD.Support for after-hours and ticket intake.

## Avoid

- "Break-fix". We are explicitly not that.
- Alarm-bell copy ("Is your business one click away from ransomware?"). Speak to confidence, not fear.
- Overly technical terms without definition on client-facing pages.
- Any SLA commitment that isn't actually honored — under-promise and over-deliver.
